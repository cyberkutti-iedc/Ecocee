# ECOCEE — TECHNOLOGY PARTNERSHIP OPERATING FLOW

## 1. PURPOSE

Ecocee operates as a technology partner, not merely as a software development vendor.

The objective of this workflow is to ensure that every client engagement follows a structured process from the initial business problem through solution discovery, engineering, deployment, handover, and long-term support.

The core principle is:

> **Understand the problem before defining the solution.
> Define the solution before defining the product.
> Define the product before beginning development.**

# 3. PHASE 01 — CLIENT ENGAGEMENT

## Objective

Establish the initial relationship and understand why the client has approached Ecocee.

## Ecocee Actions

* Welcome the client professionally.
* Establish the preferred communication channel.
* Schedule an introductory meeting or discovery call.
* Understand the client's business context.
* Identify the initial problem or opportunity.
* Avoid prematurely committing to a technical solution.

## Client Provides

* Business background.
* Current problem.
* Existing solution, if any.
* Current limitations.
* Expected outcome.
* Known budget or commercial constraints, where applicable.
* Existing technical information, where available.

## Output

**Initial Engagement Record**

No implementation commitment should be made at this stage.

---

# 4. PHASE 02 — DISCOVERY

## Objective

Understand the actual problem behind the client's stated requirement.

Ecocee should determine:

* What problem is being solved?
* Who experiences the problem?
* How is the problem currently handled?
* What is the existing technology?
* What are its limitations?
* What is the operational impact?
* What is the expected business outcome?
* What integrations are required?
* What infrastructure already exists?
* What constraints exist?
* What are the client's priorities?

## Important Rule

The client's proposed solution must not automatically be treated as the final technical solution.

Example:

Client says:

> "We need a mobile application."

Ecocee should determine:

> Why is the application required, what problem does it solve, who will use it, and whether a mobile application is actually the most appropriate solution.

## Output

**Discovery & Requirement Understanding Document**

---

# 5. PHASE 03 — BUSINESS AND TECHNICAL ASSESSMENT

## Objective

Evaluate the current situation before recommending a solution.

Ecocee evaluates:

* Existing architecture.
* Existing software.
* Existing hardware.
* Existing infrastructure.
* Existing workflows.
* Existing third-party services.
* Operational costs.
* Technology limitations.
* Scalability.
* Security considerations.
* Performance requirements.
* Integration requirements.
* Maintenance requirements.
* Potential risks.

Where applicable, Ecocee should also identify whether the existing system can be improved instead of completely replaced.

## Output

**Assessment Report**

---

# 6. PHASE 04 — SOLUTION EXPLORATION

## Objective

Explore possible approaches.

Ecocee may:

* Research technologies.
* Evaluate frameworks.
* Compare architecture options.
* Evaluate third-party services.
* Analyse infrastructure options.
* Estimate technical complexity.
* Identify risks.
* Identify dependencies.
* Explore alternative approaches.

Multiple possible solutions may be considered internally before one recommendation is presented to the client.

## Standard Turnaround

Normal solution assessment:

**3–7 working days**

Urgent assessment:

**1–3 working days where technically feasible**

The timeline should depend on complexity and information availability.

## Output

**Solution Options / Technical Direction**

---

# 7. PHASE 05 — SOLUTION RECOMMENDATION

## Objective

Recommend the most appropriate solution rather than simply the most expensive or technically complex solution.

The recommendation should consider:

* Business requirements.
* Technical feasibility.
* Cost.
* Scalability.
* Security.
* Maintainability.
* Infrastructure.
* Future expansion.
* Development effort.
* Operational requirements.

The recommendation should clearly explain:

1. What Ecocee recommends.
2. Why it is recommended.
3. How it solves the identified problem.
4. Major technologies involved.
5. Expected architecture.
6. Major risks or limitations.
7. Approximate implementation approach.

## Output

**Solution Recommendation**

Client feedback is obtained before detailed project definition.

---

# 8. PHASE 06 — PROPOSAL AND FEASIBILITY

## Objective

Convert the recommended solution into a commercially and technically understandable proposal.

The proposal should include, where applicable:

* Project objective.
* Business requirements.
* Proposed solution.
* Functional scope.
* Technical scope.
* Architecture overview.
* Integrations.
* Infrastructure requirements.
* Security considerations.
* Deliverables.
* Estimated timeline.
* Milestones.
* Commercial estimate.
* Client responsibilities.
* Ecocee responsibilities.
* Assumptions.
* Dependencies.
* Exclusions.
* Support approach.

## Output

**Solution Proposal**

---

# 9. PHASE 07 — SOW AND COMMERCIAL DEFINITION

## Objective

Convert the proposal into an executable project agreement.

The SOW should clearly define:

### Scope

What will be developed.

### Deliverables

What the client will receive.

### Milestones

How the project will be divided.

### Timeline

Expected delivery schedule.

### Commercials

Project cost and payment milestones.

### Infrastructure

Cloud, hosting, databases, APIs, storage, domains, third-party services, etc.

### Responsibilities

What Ecocee provides and what the client provides.

### Assumptions

Conditions under which the estimate is valid.

### Exclusions

Items that are explicitly outside the agreed scope.

### Acceptance Criteria

Conditions required for project acceptance.

### Change Management

Process for requirements introduced after approval.

---

# 10. PHASE 08 — CLIENT APPROVAL AND PROJECT KICK-OFF

Development does not formally begin until the required commercial and scope approvals are completed.

## Approval Checklist

* Scope approved.
* Deliverables approved.
* Milestones approved.
* Commercial terms approved.
* Infrastructure responsibilities approved.
* Client dependencies identified.
* Acceptance criteria defined.
* Change management understood.
* Required access and information available.

## Project Kick-off

Once approved:

* Conduct final kick-off meeting.
* Introduce project stakeholders.
* Confirm communication channels.
* Confirm meeting frequency.
* Confirm project milestones.
* Confirm immediate technical actions.

## Output

**Approved SOW + Project Kick-off**

# 14. PHASE 12 — CONTINUOUS CLIENT REVIEW

Client involvement should continue throughout development.

Ecocee should conduct scheduled reviews/demonstrations.

Typical review cycle:

Development
→ Internal validation
→ Client demonstration
→ Client feedback
→ Assessment
→ Approved change or correction
→ Development
→ Next review

The objective is to avoid waiting until final delivery to discover major misunderstandings.

---

# 15. PHASE 13 — MVP

Where appropriate, Ecocee should provide an early working version.

Target:

**Approximately 4 weeks**

This is a target and not a universal commitment.

The MVP should demonstrate the core business workflow rather than necessarily represent the complete product.

## MVP Review

Client evaluates:

* Core workflow.
* Usability.
* Business logic.
* Functional behaviour.
* Major integrations.

Feedback is documented and prioritised.

---

# 16. PHASE 14 — UAT AND REFINEMENT

After the major development phase:

* Ecocee prepares the release candidate.
* Client performs User Acceptance Testing.
* Issues are recorded.
* Ecocee classifies issues.
* Valid defects are corrected.
* Approved refinements are implemented.
* Final acceptance is obtained.

Important distinction:

**Defect ≠ New Requirement**

A defect is behaviour that does not satisfy the approved scope.

A new requirement is functionality that was not included in the approved scope.

New requirements may require a Change Request.

---

# 17. CHANGE REQUEST MANAGEMENT

Any material requirement introduced after SOW approval should be evaluated.

## Change Request Flow

Client Request
→ Scope Assessment
→ Technical Impact
→ Timeline Impact
→ Cost Impact
→ Client Approval
→ Implementation

Ecocee should never silently absorb significant scope changes.

Every approved change should be documented.

# 23. ECOCEE DECISION RULES

## Rule 1 — Understand Before Building

Never begin substantial development without understanding the actual business problem.

## Rule 2 — Recommend, Don't Just Execute

If the client's proposed solution is technically unsuitable, Ecocee should explain the concern and recommend an alternative.

## Rule 3 — Document Important Decisions

Important scope, technical and commercial decisions must be documented.

## Rule 4 — No Silent Scope Expansion

Material changes require evaluation and approval.

## Rule 5 — Separate Defects From New Requirements

Do not classify new functionality as a development defect.

## Rule 6 — Client Feedback Is Part of the Process

Regular feedback is expected, but feedback must be converted into documented actions.

## Rule 7 — Protect the Project Baseline

The approved SOW is the primary reference for scope, timeline and commercial boundaries.

## Rule 8 — Build for the Future

Architecture should consider maintainability, scalability, security and future expansion.

## Rule 9 — Communicate Risks Early

If Ecocee identifies a technical, commercial or timeline risk, communicate it before it becomes a project issue.

## Rule 10 — Finish Professionally

A project is not complete merely because the code works.

It is complete when:

**Build → Test → Accept → Deploy → Document → Handover → Close**
